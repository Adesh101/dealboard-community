# How to shop a lease

The goal is a car you would actually take, at a price a specific seller will honor. By the end you should have two comparable written offers, a ceiling for your counter, and a worksheet you can check against the contract.

## 1. Write six lines before opening dealer listings

Copy this brief into a private note. These are example preferences. Change them to yours.

| Decision | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Vehicle | Compact sedan, new or eligible service loaner. Premium audio required. Flexible on color. |
| Use | 36 months, 10,000 miles/year. Each extra mile matters if I drive more. |
| Budget | Up to $600 effective per month, including taxes, required fees and delivery. |
| Signing cash | $0 customer down payment. Up to $800 total out of pocket at signing. No trade. |
| Market | Register in Texas. Start within driving distance. Include distant dealers if they quote delivery. |
| Timing | Ready this week if the car is available and the written numbers hold. |

“Down” means your contribution to reduce the lease balance. Even with zero down, a dealer can ask for the first payment, fees, or a security deposit at signing. State both your down-payment instruction and your maximum cash due. Include any refundable deposit in that cash limit.

## 2. Find a benchmark and a car you could buy today

Look up the exact model, trim and drivetrain in Leasehackr SIGNED! Start with the same region and program month. Record the term, mileage, MSRP, vehicle condition, incentives and cash due. A signed deal tells you what happened on that date. An advertised special tells you to check stock and eligibility. A written quote for an available car gives you something you can act on. Keep these three labels in your notes. [Leasehackr SIGNED!](https://signed.leasehackr.com/)

Search the manufacturer's inventory and individual dealers' listings for cars that meet your brief. Open the window sticker or build sheet. Look for the **named option** you care about. A listing that says “premium sound” may still lack your preferred system. With a loaner, request its odometer, title status, in-service date, remaining warranty and the lender's allowed return mileage. Ask whether it qualifies for this month's program.

Shortlist a few actual cars at different dealerships. For each, save the stock number, equipment evidence, listing link, date checked and one sales contact in your private notes. Start with local stock. Add one out-of-area car if its seller can quote taxes for your registration location and give you a delivered price. A different model can help you decide what to buy. To ask for a price match, use a car with comparable equipment, condition and lease terms.

## 3. Ask each finalist for the same worksheet

Send the first [dealer message](dealer-messages.md) once per store. A usable quote names the car, lender, term, miles, price before incentives, eligible incentives, residual, payment including tax, itemized signing cash and any cost paid outside the lease. Note the date, expiration and whether the quoted credit tier fits you. Mark an unknown field “pending.” Ask for that one missing item before treating the quote as final.

Check the lender's residual for the specific vehicle, term and mileage. The lender assigns that value. A different mileage allowance or program can change it. The money factor helps determine the rent charge. Ask for the lender's base money factor for your tier and the factor actually quoted. If the store only gives a rent charge, retain it and request the full payment breakdown. A discount before rebates, an eligible incentive or removal of an optional product gives the seller a concrete way to improve the quote. [Federal Reserve on residual values](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/leasing/resource/consider/ongoing_info4.htm) · [Federal Reserve on rent charges](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/leasing/resource/consider/ongoing_info6.htm)

**For a Texas registration:** On a typical Texas operating lease, the lessor owes motor vehicle tax on its purchase of the car. Texas's standard rate is 6.25%. The state's tax on that purchase can affect your quote. Texas does not impose that tax on each lease payment. Ask for the dollar tax charged to you, any quoted credit and where the net amount sits on the worksheet. If the tax is already rolled into the payment, adding it again overstates cost. For an out-of-state lease brought to Texas, have the seller itemize Texas use tax and any documented credit for tax already paid elsewhere. [Texas Comptroller on leases](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/96-254/leases.php) · [Texas motor vehicle tax rate](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/96-254/tax-rates.php)

## 4. Compare the whole term and make one counter

For a normal monthly lease, add your actual cash due at signing, the scheduled payments still due after signing, and any separately paid shipping or broker fees. Subtract any deposit included in that total that you expect back in full. Include any trade equity you contribute as your money. If the return fee applies, add it to your return scenario. Divide by months for the effective monthly cost. Keep signing cash visible as a separate affordability test. Count a fee or first payment once wherever it is paid. Compare one-pay leases using their actual cash schedule.

**Worked example · fictional figures for practice.** Both dealers have the same trim and required audio. Both quote 36 months and 10,000 miles/year with taxes and dealer fees included, no down payment, no trade and no refundable deposit. Each requires only the first payment at signing. Both have the same return fee, excluded here to isolate the delivery decision.

| | Local pickup | Shipped offer |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Quoted monthly payment | $550 | $535 |
| Due at signing, first payment only | $550 | $535 |
| Remaining payments | 35 × $550 | 35 × $535 |
| Delivery paid separately | $0 | $900 |
| Total before common return fee | $19,800 | $20,160 |
| Effective monthly | **$550** | **$560** |

Local pickup wins by $360. To match it, the shipped car must come down another $10/month or the seller must cover $360 of delivery. With shipping fixed at $900, the break-even payment is **($19,800 − $900) ÷ 36 = $525**. Ask for $525 on the same terms. If the dealer offers $525 with another $1,000 down, the revised total is $20,800. Keep the local offer.

If you drive 12,000 miles a year, compare both at 12,000. If a seller only quotes 10,000, price the 6,000 extra miles over three years at that contract's per-mile charge. When you cannot confirm equipment, incentive eligibility, delivery, tax or mileage, keep that quote out of the decision until it is complete.

## 5. Check the agreement before traveling or signing

- Get the final worksheet, current availability, pickup or delivery details and deposit refund terms in writing before paying a hold.
- Match the actual car and equipment to your brief. For a loaner, match the odometer, condition, warranty start and mileage allowance to what was promised.
- Put the worksheet beside the agreement. Check term, number of payments, miles, agreed vehicle price, rebates, adjusted lease balance, residual, rent charge, payment, cash due and total payments.
- Check the itemized fees, refundable deposits, optional products, excess-mile rate, disposition fee and purchase option. Check how any trade or prior balance appears. Have any difference explained and written into the agreement before signing.
- Keep the final contract privately. For a community example, share redacted numbers, region, month and program eligibility. Label shopping targets, written quotes and signed terms accurately.

The federal lease disclosure identifies the contract figures to compare, including the payment schedule and the itemized amount due at signing. [CFPB Regulation M, § 1013.4](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1013/4/)
